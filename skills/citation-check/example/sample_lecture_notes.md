# Sample lecture notes: seagrass and coastal eutrophication

This is a practice document for the citation-check skill. It contains two
mistakes on purpose, so you can see what the report looks like when something
is wrong:

- one in-text citation (to a paper by Smith) has no entry in the reference list, and
- the seagrass depth-limit reference has the wrong volume number.

## Why seagrass is declining

Seagrass meadows are disappearing worldwide, and the rate of loss has sped up
since about 1990 (Waycott et al. 2009). Orth et al. (2006) called this a global
crisis for seagrass ecosystems.

Nutrient loading is one of the main causes. Nixon (1995) defined
eutrophication as an increase in the rate of supply of organic matter to an
ecosystem. Cloern (2001) described how the conceptual model of eutrophication
has changed over time.

Light limits how deep seagrass can grow (Duarte 1991). Some lecture notes also
claim that seagrass recovers within five years once nutrients are reduced
(Smith 2010).
